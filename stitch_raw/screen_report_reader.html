Executive Summary

Neutral-atom architectures trapped via optical tweezer arrays have demonstrated parity transition toward scalable fault-tolerant quantum computation (FTQC). Recent experimental synthesis confirms the capability to dynamically reconfigure atomic qubits mid-circuit, bypassing nearest-neighbor connectivity constraints inherent to planar superconducting lattices.

By exploiting Rydberg-state excitation protocols, dual-qubit entangling gates routinely achieve fidelities exceeding 99.5%, creating transversal entangling gates across multi-hundred atom ensembles without compounding thermal jitter. The culmination of physical hardware tests and mathematical quantum error correction (QEC) codes signifies an inflection point: neutral-atom ensembles are no longer merely quantum simulators, but viable general-purpose fault-tolerant processors.

Limitations & Epistemic Boundaries

While logical transversal CNOT operations have succeeded on 48 logical nodes, commercial readiness remains constrained by three operational hurdles:

  • Gate Duration Latency: Rydberg interaction gate times (~200 ns to 1 µs) are roughly 10x slower than superconducting microwave pulses (20-40 ns).
  • Laser Optical Wavefront Imperfections: Residual spatial variations across 2D optical spatial light modulators (SLMs) generate slight Stark shifts.
  • State-Preparation-and-Measurement (SPAM): Atom detection via fluorescence imaging still risks 0.2% heating-induced ejection.
